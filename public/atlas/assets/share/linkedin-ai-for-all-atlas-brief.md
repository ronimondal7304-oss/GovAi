# AI for All Atlas Infographic Brief

## Core Idea

Create a LinkedIn-ready infographic for the Canada AI Strategy Atlas by GovAI.fm. The image should make one idea instantly clear: AI for All is not just a government strategy document, it can be explored as a civic atlas of the questions Canadians actually ask.

The infographic should show five layers:

1. Human question personas.
2. Official AI for All actions.
3. Consultation evidence from experts and the public.
4. Country comparisons.
5. Accountability signals that separate policy, promise, law, money, and still-open delivery questions.

## Format

Primary format: LinkedIn feed image, 1080 x 1350 pixels, 4:5 aspect ratio.

The design should feel premium, civic, trustworthy, and editorial. It should not look like a generic government slide or a crypto/AI startup poster. It should look like a polished public-interest product.

## Policy vs Promise Language

Avoid saying "policy vs promise" as if they are opposites.

Better framing:

"Commitment force"

Explanation:

Policy is the overall government direction. Promise, law, money, and still open describe how much force a specific commitment appears to have.

Use these definitions:

- Law: A legal or regulatory move, such as legislation, statutory review, enforceable rules, or formal standards work.
- Money: A named funding envelope, program investment, procurement commitment, or financial instrument.
- Promise: An official commitment or direction in the strategy. It may still need law, money, timelines, or delivery rules.
- Still open: A delivery question remains, such as who owns it, when it happens, how it is enforced, or how success is measured.

Important nuance:

Tags can stack. A single action can be a promise and also have money behind it. Another can be a promise that needs future law. Another can be funded but still open because program rules or timelines are unclear.

## Country Comparison Layer

The infographic should make it clear that the atlas is not only summarizing Canada. It is also comparing Canada's approach with other countries and global norms.

Possible comparison examples to use in future versions:

- EU: hard-law AI regulation, high-risk systems, transparency duties.
- United States: agency AI governance, NIST-style risk management, sectoral approach.
- UK: pro-innovation regulation, AI safety institutions, international positioning.
- Singapore: adoption playbooks, skills, governance tools, public-sector execution.
- France: industrial policy, national champions, research and compute strategy.
- India: AI mission, compute, skills, public digital infrastructure.
- Japan: business guidelines and practical governance.
- South Korea: national AI law and competitiveness strategy.
- OECD/G7/UNESCO: global principles, voluntary codes, rights and trust baselines.

Do not crowd all countries into the poster. Use "Country comparisons" in the image, then show country-level detail inside the site or in a carousel.

## Photo Guidance

Do not use photos of all 28 experts in the main poster. It will become visually noisy and may create rights/permission issues.

Best approach:

Use small name/initial chips for experts in the first public version.

If approved headshots are available:

Use only 4 to 6 headshots max, as a small "expert voices" strip. Keep names readable. Avoid implying endorsement beyond consultation participation.

For political/ministerial photos:

Use small launch-context cards only. A sticker treatment works well: circular face crop, white rim, soft shadow, slight rotation, and a compact text card beside it. If the post is boosted, paid, or reused commercially, verify image rights and attribution requirements first.

## Visual Tone

- Deep civic green/near-black for trust and authority.
- Warm off-white background for readability.
- Controlled red accent for Canada/attention.
- Muted blue and gold for comparison/evidence signals.

Typography:

- Large, bold, editorial headline.
- Compact, high-contrast cards.
- No decorative tiny text.
- No dense paragraphs inside the image.

Composition:

- First read: headline.
- Second read: central Atlas/persona tree.
- Third read: right-side feature cards.
- Fourth read: expert/public consultation credibility band.
- Fifth read: atlas.govai.fm footer.
